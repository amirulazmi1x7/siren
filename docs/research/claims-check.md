# Pitch claims fact-check (checked 2026-11-14)

Verdicts: true / partly true / unsupported / false. "Vendor" marks industry or vendor data.

## 1. US registered nurse shortage
**Verdict: true, but shrinking.**
- HRSA, Nurse Workforce Projections (earlier edition, 2022-2037): RN shortage of 10% in 2027, 8% in 2032 and 6% in 2037 (about 208k FTE). Non-metro areas fare worse (24% vs 7% in 2027). https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/nursing-projections-factsheet.pdf
- HRSA's Dec 2025 edition (2023-2038) is reported at about 8% in 2028, falling to about 3% by 2038. This is secondhand; check it on the HRSA dashboard: https://data.hrsa.gov/topics/health-workforce/workforce-projections
- Coverage of the easing: https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/nursing/national-rn-shortage-to-shrink-lpn-demand-to-swell-report
- Evidence of easing since 2022: yes. Each newer HRSA projection shows a smaller gap, so avoid "worsening crisis" language.

**Slide:** "HRSA projects a national RN shortfall through the 2030s (about 8% in 2028), worst in non-metro areas."

## 2. ED nurse staffing is strained
**Verdict: true.**
- Survey of ED leaders (ACEP-affiliated, peer-reviewed, 2024): 98.5% reported nursing shortages, and 83.3% said they had lasted more than 12 months. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10996074/
- J Emerg Nurs analysis: among emergency nurses who left, 64% cited burnout and 56% cited staffing. https://hspop.uw.edu/burnout-staffing-and-stress
- NSI 2025 retention report (vendor/industry): emergency RN turnover was 19.1% (2024 data). https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/quality/nursing/nurse-turnover-rates-by-specialty/

**Slide:** "98.5% of surveyed ED leaders report nursing shortages; ED RN turnover is about 19% a year."

## 3. Thin hospital margins
**Verdict: true.**
- Kaufman Hall National Hospital Flash Report, Dec 2025 (consultancy data): median calendar-year 2025 operating margin was 1.3% including allocations. https://www.kaufmanhall.com/sites/default/files/2026-02/KH-NHFR_Report_December2025_Metrics.pdf and https://www.kaufmanhall.com/news/hospitals-face-2026-new-normal-rising-expenses-and-shifts-revenue-mix
- No safety-net-specific figure was verified. Cite the median only, or add an AHA or MedPAC source before claiming anything about safety-net hospitals.

**Slide:** "The median US hospital ran a 1.3% operating margin in 2025 (Kaufman Hall)."

## 4. Travel nurse about $90/hr vs about $56/hr for employed RNs
**Verdict: partly true.** The $90 figure holds up. The $56 figure is unsupported as stated.
- SIA/NATHO benchmarking (vendor/industry survey): aggregate travel RN bill rate of $90.13/hr in 2024 and $90.54/hr in 2025. Reported via https://nursa.com/facility/articles/how-much-do-travel-nurses-cost (vendor).
- BLS OEWS May 2023: RN mean wage $45.42/hr ($94,480/yr). https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes291141.htm
- The May 2024 mean is about $47.32/hr according to secondary sites. Verify it at https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes291141.htm
- No source for $56/hr was found. It might be wage plus some benefits, but that is unconfirmed.
- The comparison mixes measures. A bill rate includes the agency margin, so compare it with the fully loaded staff cost, not the wage. NURSA (vendor) puts the fully loaded cost of a hospital staff RN at about $80/hr, a premium of only about 13%.

**Slide:** "Agency travel RNs bill about $90/hr (SIA/NATHO 2025), roughly 2x the $45-47/hr mean staff RN wage (BLS)." Footnote: bill rate vs wage, not fully loaded cost.

## 5. Offload delays: California, Ontario, NHS England
**Verdict: true for all three.**
- **California:** AB 40 (2023, chaptered) requires each local EMS agency to adopt an ambulance patient offload time standard of no more than 30 minutes, 90% of the time, by July 1, 2024. EMSA sets the statewide method under Health and Safety Code 1797.120. Sources: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB40/2023 and https://emsa.ca.gov/apot/
  - LA County sets 30 min/90%: https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/dhs/1179840_505APOT.pdf
  - Sacramento steps down to 20 min by 2027: https://dhs.saccounty.gov/PUB/EMS/Documents/PoliciesProceduresProtocols/2000/PP-2521%20Ambulance%20Patient%20Offload%20Time%20(APOT).pdf
- **Ontario:** The Ministry of Health has funded the Dedicated Offload Nurses Program since 2008-09. In Sept 2023 it added $51M over 3 years for 30 municipalities, about 800k offload hours. Sources: https://samoosterhoffmpp.ca/ontario-increasing-funding-for-dedicated-offload-nursing-program-in-niagara/ (MPP release) and https://rmh.org/news-releases/ontario-ministry-of-health-to-provide-up-to-195-000-in-funding-for-a-dedicated-offload-nurse-program. A news.ontario.ca primary page was not located.
- **NHS England:** The Urgent and Emergency Care Plan 2025/26 requires a "maximum 45-minute ambulance handover time standard" to put about 550,000 more ambulances back on the road. The 15-minute target still applies, and crews release only when it is safe. https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/urgent-and-emergency-care-plan-2025-26

**Slide:** "Others regulate offload delays: California caps them at 30 min (90%), Ontario funds dedicated offload nurses, NHS England enforces a 45-min handover maximum."

## 6. Ambulance cost of about $150 per unit-hour
**Verdict: unsupported. No credible single US benchmark was found.**
- Figures vary widely. An EMS World worked example gives about $79/unit-hour (illustrative, not measured): https://www.hmpgloballearningnetwork.com/site/emsworld/feature-story/determining-costs-and-ems-system-and-why-it-matters-part-2
- An EMS1 analysis uses $220/unit-hour: https://www.ems1.com/ems-trend-report/quantifying-the-gap-between-expenses-and-revenue-for-ems-services
- San Diego 2025 study: about $658,511/yr per paid 24/7 unit, or about $75/hr. https://timesofsandiego.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/San-Diego-Phase-2-Report-FINAL-AP-Triton-Feasibility-Study-1.pdf
- $150 sits inside this roughly $75-220 range but cannot be cited to a source.

**Slide:** "Ambulance unit-hours cost an estimated $75-220 each, varying by system" (or present $150 as a stated model assumption).

## 7. Offload nurse cost of about $90/hr ($180 per 2-hour shift)
**Verdict: true as an estimate.** It sits between the fully loaded cost of a staff RN in NYC and overtime pay. Checked 2026-11-14 night (Person B).
- **Wage:** BLS OEWS May 2023, New York-Newark-Jersey City metro, registered nurses: mean **$53.47/hr** ($111,210/yr). This is the latest metro figure confirmed; the May 2024 metro figure was not verified. https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes291141.htm
- **Fully loaded cost:** BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation, registered nurses in hospitals (Dec 2024): wages are **64.2%** of total compensation (the rest is benefits, paid leave, payroll taxes). $53.47 / 0.642 ≈ **$83/hr** to the hospital at regular pay. https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2025/charting-compensation-costs-during-national-nurses-month.htm
  - Context: all hospital workers averaged $67.64/hr in total compensation in June 2024, with wages 66.1% of it. https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/compensation-costs-67-64-per-hour-in-hospitals-june-2024.htm
- **If the shift is extra hours (likely for an add-on offload shift):** overtime at 1.5x wage plus the same benefits ≈ **$105-110/hr**. Agency/travel RN bill rates ≈ **$90/hr** (SIA/NATHO, vendor data, see section 4).
- **Union pay context:** NYC Health + Hospitals RN starting salary rose to about **$106,000/yr** under the 2023 NYSNA contract (union announcement as reported). https://www.amny.com/news/nyc-public-nurses-win-historic-pay-bump-in-new-union-contract and https://www.nysna.org/press/2023/nyc-public-hospital-nurses-win-historic-contract-pay-parity-and-safe-staffing
- **Real program cross-check (floor, not a NYC estimate):** Ontario committed CA$51M over 3 years (Aug 2023) for about 800,000 dedicated offload hours, about **CA$64/hr** (≈ US$46). Canadian wages and staffing mix are lower. https://rmh.org/news-releases/ontario-ministry-of-health-to-provide-up-to-195-000-in-funding-for-a-dedicated-offload-nurse-program
- **What the model assumes the nurse does** (`sim/model.py`): takes up to 4 patients at once off crews during the 2-hour block; each crew is released after 20 min. These two numbers are assumptions, not fitted to data.

**Slide:** "An offload nurse costs the hospital about $83-110/hr in NYC (BLS wage + benefits; overtime at the top end), so a 2-hour shift is about $165-220." Footnote: the page uses $180.
**Weaker point to watch:** the ambulance cost ($220/unit-hour, section 6 range $75-220) drives most of the "nurses are cheaper" gap, not the nurse cost.
